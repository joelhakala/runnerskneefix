## General feedback

- The first seconds often do not make clear that this is for runners.
- A person can consciously look at one place at a time. Too much text at once cannot be read.
- Design the eye's path through the animation; keep conscious attention on one point at a time.
- Respect the platform safe zones so nothing essential sits under app UI.
- Text in two places at once, or text that is missed, breaks the context and adds friction.

## Patterns (count of videos)

1. Runner and knee context missing or weak in the hook: general, 110, 111 (and implied by praise for 105).
2. More than one thing to read or watch at once: general, 112, 108, 107, 105.
3. A visual vocabulary that is used before it is taught: 101, 102.
4. A term or claim used before it is explained: 109 (alarm), 104 (hook), 108 (hook wording).
5. Stiff running figure: 103 (also affects 108, 112).
6. Data shown without a title that states the point: 105.
7. Payoff incomplete: 109.
8. Weak visual concept: 106, 107 orientation.

## What the owner liked (keep)

- 105: a quoted belief as the hook plus one clear physical metaphor.
- 110: one concrete number from one's own training, simple bars.
- 112: a real study told as a short story.

## My read (labelled as interpretation)

- My design rule "labels next to what they name" produced text in several places. The owner's eye-flow rule outranks it:
  one reading position, and the animation points at things instead of labelling them all.
- The shared vocabulary (two lines, stack, alarm) was meant to pay off across videos, but each video is seen alone.
  Every video must teach its own visual in the first seconds, or use a physical metaphor that needs no teaching (tyre).
- The strongest video (105) is also the one with the least abstraction. That may be the main lesson of this round.
